The support bottleneck

The inbox was doing work the system should have handled first.

As operations scaled, the queue filled with the same micro questions: order status, fulfillment timing, returns, stock checks, tracking, split shipments, customs, and product details. Every one of those questions looked small by itself. Together, they consumed the same attention needed for unusual orders, upset customers, and problems that could not be solved with a standard answer.

The goal was not to hide humans behind a chatbot. It was to build a useful buffer that could recognize repeatable needs, surface a precise answer quickly, and escalate cleanly when the situation actually needed a person.

01 · Response lag

02 · Operational fatigue

What I built

Four connected changes turned support into a system.

The useful part was not any one feature. The payoff came from making the interface, the content, the AI behavior, and the routing logic speak the same language.

01 · Beacon redesign

Self service before contact

I reconfigured the Help Scout Beacon so customers encountered common topics and search before a blank contact box. The first interaction became guided instead of open ended.

02 · Knowledge architecture

Micro docs instead of giant FAQ walls

I broke broad FAQ content into single topic documents designed for fast scanning and cleaner AI retrieval. One question, one useful answer, one obvious next step.

03 · AI guardrails

Short, direct, brand aware replies

I shaped AI instructions around concise answers, plain language, brand voice, and no vague customer service filler. The system had permission to be helpful without sounding manufactured.

04 · Workflow routing

Keywords became actions

I built Help Scout workflows around recurring terms such as tracking, shipping, and policy so the system could surface portals, update status, apply tags, and create cleaner analytics automatically.

Operational shift

Same customers. Far less unnecessary queue time.

What I owned

The outcome

A support system that knew when not to become a ticket.

The work moved repeatable questions out of the manual queue, made answers easier to retrieve, and protected human support capacity for the moments that needed context, flexibility, and emotional intelligence. It also created a cleaner bridge between customer service, ecommerce operations, and the information customers saw before they ever wrote in.
