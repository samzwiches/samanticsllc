Support does not stop at the prison gate.

The problem worth solving

People in crisis should not need insider knowledge to find a place to begin.

Families often meet the system through a rushed phone call, a transfer, a release date, or a sudden need for housing and identification. Information is scattered, eligibility changes, and shame can make asking for help even harder.

I designed Outside Inmates around real moments rather than bureaucratic categories. Visitors can choose the situation closest to theirs, follow a calm guided path, search practical resources, and understand where the platform’s help ends and professional support should begin.

Design approach

Calm enough for crisis. Human enough to trust.

Deep blue green creates steadiness, warm paper tones soften the institutional subject matter, and clay red guides attention without turning urgency into alarm. The design avoids prison imagery, pity, and sensationalism.

Information design

Smaller decisions

Long, overwhelming systems are broken into one first action, a few clear steps, reminders, checklists, and related resources.

Trust and safety

Boundaries belong in the UX

Provider details require confirmation. Legal and clinical limits are stated plainly. Users are reminded to protect information, money, and personal safety.

Voice

“You do not need to understand the system before you begin.”

Product architecture

Designed around moments, needs, and the next useful action.

The platform connects guided journeys, resource data, family support, reentry planning, community questions, and careful trust language.

The outcome

A compassionate product with serious operational bones.

Outside Inmates demonstrates that mission driven design can be warm without becoming vague and useful without becoming cold. It connects human services thinking, marketing, content, graphic design, UX, resource research, and technical systems in one coherent product.
