The assignment I gave myself

Build the horse world a front door without sanding off its personality.

The information people need is scattered across show websites, private groups, text chains, barn gossip, listing pages, and old fashioned word of mouth. The product had to organize that chaos while still feeling unmistakably written by someone who has actually stood at an in gate.

This was not a logo dropped onto a template. It became a complete brand and product system with navigation, reusable content patterns, marketplace structures, account flows, editable page media, membership, and a voice that can be polished without becoming precious.

Visual direction

Editorial polish with barn dirt still on its boots.

The identity uses quiet equestrian colors, strong editorial typography, generous space, and crisp utility. Oxblood gives it authority. Hunter green keeps it grounded. Cream and weathered neutrals prevent the experience from feeling like another loud sales platform.

Behind the pretty parts

I built tools so the brand can keep changing without quietly breaking itself.

The administrative media system supports page specific imagery, focal point control, overlay tones, custom colors, and reusable section appearance settings. That means future content can evolve without turning every update into a developer emergency.

Strategy, design, language, structure, and the machinery underneath.

The outcome

A working platform foundation with a recognizable soul.

The result is a live, responsive product that makes a large idea feel understandable. It demonstrates how I connect branding, marketing, graphic design, UX, content strategy, customer thinking, and operational systems instead of treating them as unrelated departments trapped in separate little boxes.
